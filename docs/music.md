# Music

The Music tab is listening stats and auto-updating Spotify playlists, in the style of stats.fm. It has three parts:

- **Stats.** Minutes listened, top songs, top artists, top genres, listening over time and recent plays, for 7 days, 30 days, 90 days, 6 months, a year, 5 years, all time or custom dates. Everything ranks by minutes listened; plays are shown second. **Genre** filters the page to artists with a matching genre: a word matches whole words, so "house" covers deep house and tech house but not warehouse, and clicking a genre in Top genres applies it. A play counts toward its lead artist's genres. A play counts once 30 seconds of it were heard. Versions of one recording count together: album, single, deluxe, explicit and clean, and remasters. Any other label on a title (live, acoustic, remix, demo, "Taylor's Version", sped up, slowed and so on) makes it a song of its own, so an unfamiliar label never merges two versions by mistake.
- **Artist view.** Click an artist anywhere on the Stats tab (a row in Top artists, the Top artist card, or the artist under a song or a recent play) for that artist's stats over the period you picked: minutes and plays, their rank among your artists and share of your listening, first and last play in the period and your first play ever, listening over time, and their top songs. A play counts toward the song's first-credited artist only, the same rule Top artists uses, so a featured artist gets no credit and clicking "Kendrick Lamar, Jay Rock" opens Kendrick Lamar. The genre filter doesn't narrow it. It works on the phone too, from the cloud's copy of your history.
- **Synced playlists.** Each rule is a name, how many songs (1–500), the period, an optional genre, and whether and how often it updates (hourly to every 90 days). **Preview** shows exactly what would be written. Jarvis only creates new private playlists and only ever writes to the ones it created; stopping a rule leaves its playlist in Spotify. A song appears at most once, as the version you played most in the period, checked as playable in your country. If that version isn't playable, the next one you played is used, then an exact-match search; if nothing fits, the song is skipped, the next one fills its place, and the card lists what was skipped and why. A list that hasn't changed isn't rewritten. If you delete one of these playlists in Spotify, the card says so and **Recreate** makes a new one. Songs rank by minutes, as on the Stats tab. A rule with fixed dates is built once and never updated, since its songs can't change. Rules live in the plan, so the phone can create and edit them.
- **Setup.** History and tracking.

**History.** Jarvis records your last plays from Spotify every 10 minutes while the desktop runs. Spotify only reports the last 50, so a long gap with the computer off can lose plays; the cloud fills that (below), and the Setup tab lists any gap it couldn't. Older history comes from exports, imported on the desktop under **Music → Setup → Import listening history**: choose the folder holding a Spotify *Extended streaming history* export (`Streaming_History_Audio_*.json`) and/or an Apple Music *Apple Media Services information* export (it reads `Apple Music - Play History Daily Tracks.csv`). The files are read in place and nothing is copied; parsed plays go to `data/music/*.local.json`, which is never committed or served. **Check** shows what an import would do before **Import** applies it. A stream found in two files (an old and a new export unzipped side by side) counts once. A new export is the record for the dates it covers; plays from the previous export outside those dates are kept, so a shorter export never deletes history, and the check warns when an export ends earlier than the one already imported. A Spotify export also replaces the plays Jarvis recorded inside its dates, since it has exact listen times; a recorded play of the same track within 10 minutes after the export ends counts once. When a newer export arrives, unzip it anywhere under the folder you imported before and import that folder again.

**Minutes.** Spotify exports give the exact time heard. Apple's daily export gives a total per row, split across its plays. Plays Jarvis records count the song's full length, until an export replaces them. Exports imported before minutes existed are estimated from song length until they are imported again.

**Artwork** comes from Spotify and **genres** from [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org), since Spotify no longer gives genres to apps like this one. The desktop looks them up in the background while it runs, most-listened artists first, and refreshes them every 90 days; the phone and website get them from the desktop. An artist is matched in MusicBrainz by its Spotify link, or by name only when exactly one artist has that exact name; with no sure match it gets no genres rather than a guess. The first pass over a large history takes days, since each artist needs a Spotify lookup first and those are rationed (below); until then a genre filter only includes artists already looked up, and the page says how much of your listening has genres. MusicBrainz is asked for an artist's Spotify ID or name and nothing else. Apple's export only gives the day and hour of each play. Apple songs are matched to Spotify by an exact title and artist search when they rank into a playlist.

**Playlist syncs come first.** Spotify limits how often one app can ask, across every computer and the cloud together, so artwork and song details only use what syncs leave. Lookups take at most about 60 Spotify requests an hour per computer (20 per hourly cloud check), don't start in the 15 minutes before a playlist is due, and stop the moment a sync needs Spotify. If Spotify pushes back, syncs wait exactly as long as Spotify says and are retried right then, while lookups wait at least six hours, or twice Spotify's wait. So stats and artwork can lag behind, and **Music → Setup** says when they're waiting. MusicBrainz is a separate service and keeps its own pace of about one request a second.

**Cloud tracking without the computer on.** In **Music → Setup** on jarvis.example.com:

1. In your Spotify app's settings ([developer.spotify.com/dashboard](https://developer.spotify.com/dashboard)), add the redirect URI `https://jarvis.example.com/api/music/spotify/callback`, then **Connect Spotify** on the site. This is a separate grant from the desktop's, stored encrypted in the database.
2. **Set up the hourly check** shows a URL with a token once. At [cron-job.org](https://cron-job.org) (free), create a job that calls it every hour. Each call records new plays and updates due playlists, within about 40 seconds.

A connected desktop sends its imports and plays to the cloud and pulls back what the cloud recorded, so both have the full history. While the cloud can't be reached, the desktop doesn't update playlists, so it never writes a list missing the cloud's plays.
